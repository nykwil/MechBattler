Prototype · rev A

Mobile builder

A working prototype, not a picture of one. Real chassis masks and the real 23-part catalog from packages/sim. Place parts, rotate them, break the power bus, read the faults.

On a phone this fills the screen. That is the only way to judge whether the controls fall under a thumb.

The interaction under test

Tap a part to arm it — that does not place it. A ghost appears on the plate. Tap any cell to move the ghost, then Cancel, Rotate, or Place.

Your fingertip covers the cells you aim at, so drag-to-place fights the puzzle. Tap-then-confirm keeps the target visible and gives a moment to back out.

Detach — one state, not two

A selected part offers exactly one action: Detach. It comes off the plate and you are back in the placement state holding it — move, rotate, place. Rotate-in-place and remove-in-place are gone; detach-then-rotate and detach-then-discard already did both jobs, in a state that existed anyway.

Because the part is already off the plate, backing out has nothing to return to — so the button reads Discard rather than Cancel, and turns red. Worth a decision: Esc discards too, and there is no undo.

Next match, always visible

The strip above Parts names who you are building against. Tap it for intel: threat, chassis, engagement range, and confirmed parts — partial by design. Elites telegraph the mod they carry.

It sits on the build screen rather than a separate page because the loop is read the opponent → move a radiator → read it again. Making that a navigation costs you the loop.

Bench — why a gun is silent

The readout sheet has a Bench tab. The range table is the useful part: real falloff bands from the catalog, and when a weapon contributes nothing it says why — range or power — instead of printing a zero.

Strand a gun off the bus and watch the whole column change. Burst DPS and the falloff maths here are exact; sustained DPS and hit fraction need the real 60-second run, and the panel says so rather than inventing a number.

Why there is no nudge pad

An earlier pass had a four-way pad next to Rotate. Tapping a cell already positions the ghost, so the arrows were a second control doing one job — and they crowded the row below --tap-min. Cut.

Arrow keys still nudge, because a keyboard has no cell to tap. Tab to the grid, then ←↑↓→ to move, R to rotate, Enter to place or select, Esc to cancel. One model serves touch, keyboard, and screen reader — the current SVG grid serves none of them.

Reading power and heat

Parts · Power · Heat at the top right recolours the plate. Power shows what reaches the core and what is stranded; Heat shows what makes it and what sheds it.

The readout bar is a button — tap it for gauges and the full fault list. It carried no label or caret in the first pass and nobody found it, which is the affordance failing, not the feature.

Room for part art

Each cell is a container, not a painted square: the category fill is one layer, so a sprite or a rendered part can sit on top later without changing the layout, the hit targets, or the accessible names. The grid stays canonical while the catalog is still moving.

One part, one shape

Cells belonging to the same part share edges and carry a single outline, so a 3×3 Furnace reads as one object rather than nine squares. An earlier pass put a 1 px gutter between every cell and multi-cell parts fell apart visually.

This is what .part-outline does with boundary paths in the desktop editor; here it is per-edge box shadows, which keeps every cell an independent tap target and focus stop.

State without colour

Unpowered parts are hatched as well as dimmed, so the fault reads for colour-blind players and in sunlight. Colour is never the only channel.

Numbers

Mass and power draw are the catalog's real values. Heat and connectivity are simplified for the prototype — the authoritative model lives in packages/sim. This page is for judging layout and interaction, not for balance.
