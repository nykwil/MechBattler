Command on a phone

A live battle you can actually command. Real chassis speeds, real weapon bands, arcs and cycle times from catalog.ts, a 20 Hz tick and a 240 m arena like the sim. The resolution model is simplified — this is for judging the HUD, not balance.

Console below, map above

The panel at the bottom is the mech's instrument console — every gauge lives there, on a physical-feeling surface with a lit top bevel. The glass above it is the windshield, except what it shows is an overhead map.

So the glass carries only marks that belong on a map: terrain, both mechs, your waypoint, per-gun reach envelopes, and the target lock with range. Heat, damage, hull and mass all moved down into the console where instruments belong.

The damage vignette went with them. It was justified as "edge of vision", but the view is a map, not your eyes — a red rim on a tactical display makes no sense. Damage is now a bar you can actually read a number off.

The grid is the terrain grid

The old lines were drawn every 50 m over terrain patches that sat on no grid at all — they looked like a lattice and lined up with nothing. Terrain now sits on the sim's own TERRAIN_CELL_SIZE_M = 20 lattice and the lines mark exactly those cells, at about a fifth of their old contrast.

There is no ammo

U-AMMO "Bin (ammo store)" is in the catalog but explicitly disabled, and audit.ts enforces it: "U-AMMO must remain disabled until ammunition is functional." diversity.ts calls it a dead placeholder — ballistics do not consume ammo until Track C lands.

So there are no per-gun counts to show, and inventing them would put a mechanic on screen the game does not have. The chip layout leaves room for a count when Track C ships.

What mass actually is

Hull is structural HP left. Mass is the fraction of part mass still working — losing the 650 kg Maul costs far more than the 100 kg Gill, which is why it is a different number.

It earns a hairline rather than a headline because it changes exactly one thing: at combat.ts:1524, a battle that reaches the time limit is awarded to whoever has more functional mass. Real, but narrow.

Which guns can reach

Each gun draws its own envelope on the glass — an annular wedge spanning its arc between rangeStart and rangeEnd. It goes green when that gun can actually fire. The Judge reaches 50–150 m through a 60° arc; the Maul only 15–45 m through 45°, so its wedge is a tight patch right in front of you.

Each chip also carries the live range to target against that gun's own band, and how far out of it you are — so RANGE tells you which way to move, not merely that you cannot shoot.

Heat runs the full console

What you need from heat is headroom before cutout, and a column shows headroom at a glance in a way a horizontal bar never does — so it runs the whole height of the console. The taller the thermometer, the finer you can read the gap to the 130 °C line. Marks at 100 and 130; underneath, the risk in words — seconds to cutout at the current rate, not just a temperature.

Nothing resizes with its text

Every slot whose contents change is now a fixed box: the gun state label, the range hint, the clock, the core and hull values, the Auto toggle, the heat readout, the lost-parts line. A HUD that reflows as numbers tick is a HUD you cannot read at a glance.

Where the words could not fit the box, the words changed. DESTROYED was nine characters and blew out a slot sized for READY — it is DEAD now. Every state label is five characters or fewer, so the whole set shares one 62 px slot and the gun row can never shuffle.

The damage widget

Left of the console is your actual mech: the CH-5 Mule silhouette with your parts placed on it, each fading as it takes damage and going hatched red when destroyed. Damage lands on parts, so losing the Judge really does take the Judge offline — its chip flips to DESTROYED.

It sits in the console beside a plain Core bar, because "how close am I to dying" deserves a number and a bar, not just a fading square. Tap the mech for gauges and the log.

Movement is the sim's, not an approximation

The three speeds really are different — the Mule does 6.0 forward, 4.0 sideways, 3.0 backwards. Max speed for any heading comes off an ellipse through those three points, which turns out to be exactly what combat.ts already does in maxSpeedInDirection. Throttle scales that ceiling by the sim's own fractions: creep 0.30, cruise 0.65, flank 1.00.

Mechs now carry a velocity and accelerate into it at the chassis' 3.0 m/s², and turn at 90°/s. Crossing speed has to be a real vector for any of the gunnery to mean anything.

Three penalties, all real

Your own speed adds 0.3 mrad per m/s of dispersion. The target's crossing speed — only the component across the line of sight, never closing speed — becomes lead error over the time of flight plus 0.3 s of fire-control lag. Range multiplies dispersion into metres.

Those combine as sigma = hypot(dispersion, lead), and hit chance is the error function of the target's half-width over that. A round's scatter is its miss — one number, not a visual and a separate dice roll.

Rounds have flight time

Projectile speeds are the catalog's: Judge 600 m/s, Maul 400, Needle 900. A Judge shot takes a fifth of a second to cross 120 m, so you can see which way it is going, and slow rounds are genuinely dodgeable by a crossing target while hitscan pays only the lag share.

The ƒx panel

Top bar, next to pause. It draws the speed envelope with your heading and actual velocity on it, then every term of the shot: your speed and the jitter it buys, dispersion in mrad and metres, the target's crossing speed, time of flight plus lag, lead error, combined sigma, and the hit chance that falls out.

Pause and step through it — that is the fastest way to see why a shot missed.

Face target

A toggle beside the throttle, greyed out under autopilot like the throttle is. Face on keeps the guns tracking the enemy. Face off points the mech where it is going — so retreating turns your guns away with you.

It measurably changes the fight: running for the far corner with Face on, the guns spend zero time out of arc; with Face off they are locked out by ARC 43% of the time.

Auto owns movement and throttle

One toggle, not three verbs. Auto on: the autopilot has position and speed, the throttle segment greys out, and the glass does not take waypoints. Auto off: both are yours — tap where you want to go, or tap where you already are to stand still. There is no separate Hold.

The real movement model inverted this fight. Charging in at flank used to win; now it loses, because 5.4 m/s of your own speed buys 1.6 mrad of extra dispersion and you miss on the way in. Sitting still and letting the autopilot hold its band wins at about 22 s. Speed is no longer free — which is the whole point of giving the player a throttle.

The problem being solved

The desktop battle HUD is mouse-shaped in three ways that do not survive contact with a phone: right-click reverts move orders to auto, hover reveals what a weapon does, and weapon hold/free is a click on a small slot.

Guns say why they are silent

On desktop, RANGE / ARC / HOT are tiny marks on a slot, explained by a hover blurb you have to go looking for. Here that reason is the chip's content — always visible, never hovered.

Watch the Judge flip between RANGE and READY as you close. Tap a gun to hold it; the reason becomes HOLD, in amber, because that one is your decision and not the mech's.

No right-click, so name the verb

Tapping the arena sets a waypoint. Auto hands movement back to the autopilot and Hold plants the mech. Three named buttons instead of one overloaded gesture — and the current mode is always readable, which right-click never was.

Pause is a first-class control

You cannot issue orders on a phone as fast as with a mouse, so pause sits in the top bar at full size rather than being a spacebar. The sim is deterministic and tick-based, so pausing costs nothing and thinking is free. Orders given while paused apply on resume.

Same shell as the builder

Topbar, hero, command bar, sheet. The arena is the plate; the gun row is the armed controls. Everything is ≥44 px, the damage widget carries a caret because it opens something, and Esc closes the sheet with focus restored.
